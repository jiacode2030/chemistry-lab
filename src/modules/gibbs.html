Gibbs free energy & spontaneity

Two forces decide whether a process happens on its own: the drive to release energy and the drive to spread out. Gibbs free energy adds them up, and temperature decides who wins.

The idea

Ice melts above 0 °C and freezes below it. The energy side says "stay frozen" (melting costs heat) while the disorder side says "melt" (liquid has more entropy). At low temperature energy wins; at high temperature disorder wins; at 0 °C they tie. Temperature multiplies the entropy term, so heat helps entropy-driven processes.

That tug-of-war is ΔG = ΔH − TΔS. A process is thermodynamically favorable (spontaneous) when ΔG < 0, at equilibrium when ΔG = 0, and unfavorable when ΔG > 0. The signs of ΔH and ΔS give four cases: (−, +) always favorable; (+, −) never; (−, −) favorable only at low T; (+, +) favorable only at high T. Where the sign flips is at T = ΔH/ΔS. Note "spontaneous" says nothing about speed: diamond turning into graphite is favorable but takes forever (the activation energy is huge).

Try this

Predict where it flips, then slide the temperature.

  1. Melt ice: at what temperature does ΔG change sign?

    What you should see

    At about 273 K (0 °C), where T = ΔH/ΔS = 6010/22.0. Below that ΔG is positive (ice is stable); above it, negative (melting is favorable).

  2. Make ammonia. Is it better to run the reaction hot or cold, thermodynamically?

    What you should see

    Cold: ΔH is negative and ΔS is negative, so the reaction is favorable only below about 464 K. (Industry runs it hot anyway to make it fast, and uses pressure to compensate.)

  3. Choose ozone formation, 3O₂ → 2O₃. Does any temperature work?

    What you should see

    No: ΔH is positive and ΔS is negative, so ΔG is positive at every temperature. It never happens spontaneously (UV light provides the energy instead).

Worked example

Lime kilns heat limestone to make quicklime for cement: CaCO₃(s) → CaO(s) + CO₂(g), ΔH° = +178.3 kJ and ΔS° = +160.6 J/K. (a) Is it favorable at 298 K? (b) At what temperature does it become favorable?

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Units first. ΔS° = 160.6 J/K = 0.1606 kJ/K, so it matches ΔH in kJ.
  2. (a) At 298 K. ΔG° = 178.3 − (298)(0.1606) = 178.3 − 47.9 = +130.4 kJ. Positive: not favorable at room temperature (limestone doesn't decompose on its own).
  3. (b) Find the flip. ΔG° = 0 when T = ΔH/ΔS = 178.3 / 0.1606 = 1110 K (about 837 °C).
  4. Interpret. Both ΔH and ΔS are positive, so the reaction is favorable above 1110 K, where the entropy term (the CO₂ gas) outweighs the energy cost. This is why lime kilns run hot.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing units: ΔH in kJ and ΔS in J/K. Convert ΔS to kJ/K (divide by 1000) before multiplying by T.
  • Using °C instead of kelvin for T.
  • Equating "spontaneous" with "fast" or "happens quickly". It only means thermodynamically favorable.
  • Saying ΔG° > 0 means no reaction at all. It means the equilibrium lies toward reactants (K < 1); a little product still forms.
  • Confusing ΔG° with ΔG. ΔG° is for standard conditions (Q = 1); ΔG depends on Q and is zero at equilibrium.

Thermodynamic vs kinetic

ΔG tells you the direction a system will go and how far (through K). The activation energy tells you how fast. A reaction with large negative ΔG but a high barrier (diamond to graphite, H₂ + O₂ → H₂O at room temperature) can sit for ages until a spark or catalyst helps.

Thermodynamic vs kinetic control, and coupled reactions

ΔG tells you whether a reaction can go, not how fast. A reaction can be thermodynamically favorable (ΔG < 0) but kinetically slow because of a large activation energy: diamond → graphite, or H₂ + O₂ → H₂O at room temperature. When a reaction can give two products, the one that forms fastest (lower Ea) is the kinetic product; the one that is more stable (lower ΔG) is the thermodynamic product, which wins if the reaction is reversible and given enough time.

An unfavorable reaction (ΔG > 0) can be driven by coupling it to a favorable one when the two share an intermediate. The overall ΔG is the sum: ΔGtotal = ΔG₁ + ΔG₂. If the sum is negative, the pair proceeds. Cells do this constantly, using ATP → ADP + Pᵢ (ΔG° ≈ −30 kJ/mol) to drive reactions that would not go alone. You can also compute ΔG° = ΣnΔG°f(products) − ΣnΔG°f(reactants).
