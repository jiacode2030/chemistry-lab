Composition & empirical formulas

Weigh the pieces of a compound and you can work out its formula. The trick is that formulas count atoms, and scales measure mass, so you have to convert through moles.

The idea

A fruit salad is 60 g apples and 30 g grapes. Is that 2 apples to 1 grape? Not at all, because an apple weighs far more than a grape. To find the count ratio you divide each mass by the mass of one piece. For atoms, that is the molar mass, so grams become moles.

The empirical formula is the simplest whole-number ratio of atoms. The molecular formula is the real count in one molecule, which is a whole-number multiple of the empirical formula. Glucose is C₆H₁₂O₆, but its empirical formula is just CH₂O.

From percent to formula

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. Hydrogen peroxide is 5.93% H and 94.07% O by mass. Is the formula HO₁₆? Walk through the steps.

    What you should see

    In 100 g there is 5.88 mol H and 5.88 mol O: a 1 : 1 ratio, so HO is the empirical formula. Mass percent and atom count are not the same thing: oxygen is the bigger share of the mass but only equal in count.

  2. Iron(III) oxide gives a ratio of 1 : 1.5. What do you do next?

    What you should see

    You can't have 1.5 atoms, so multiply both by 2 to get the whole numbers 2 : 3, giving Fe₂O₃. Ratios ending in .5, .33 or .25 call for multiplying by 2, 3 or 4.

  3. Octane is 84.1% C and 15.9% H. How far do you have to multiply?

    What you should see

    The ratio is 1 : 2.25, so multiply by 4 to get 4 : 9: the empirical formula is C₄H₉. The actual molecular formula, C₈H₁₈, needs the molar mass to decide.

Worked example

A compound is 40.0% C, 6.71% H and 53.3% O by mass, and its molar mass is 180 g/mol. Find the empirical and molecular formulas.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. Assume 100 g. The percentages become grams: 40.0 g C, 6.71 g H, 53.3 g O.
  2. Grams to moles. C: 40.0 / 12.01 = 3.33 mol. H: 6.71 / 1.008 = 6.66 mol. O: 53.3 / 16.00 = 3.33 mol.
  3. Divide by the smallest (3.33). C : H : O = 1.00 : 2.00 : 1.00. They are already whole numbers.
  4. Empirical formula. CH₂O, with a mass of 12.01 + 2(1.008) + 16.00 = 30.03 g/mol.
  5. Molecular formula. 180 / 30.03 = 6.0, so multiply everything by 6: C₆H₁₂O₆.

Common mistakes

  • Using the mass ratio as if it were the atom ratio. Always convert grams to moles first.
  • Rounding 1.5 or 1.33 to 1 or 2. Multiply by 2 or 3 instead; only round values within about 0.1 of a whole number.
  • Giving the empirical formula when the question asks for the molecular formula (you need the molar mass).
  • Forgetting to divide by the smallest number of moles, not the largest.
  • Dividing the molar mass by the wrong thing. Use the molar mass of the empirical formula, not of one element.

The recipe

(1) Assume 100 g so percent becomes grams. (2) Convert each to moles. (3) Divide by the smallest. (4) If any value isn't close to a whole number, multiply all of them by the same small integer. (5) The whole numbers are subscripts in the empirical formula. (6) For the molecular formula, divide the molar mass by the empirical formula mass to get a whole number n, and multiply the subscripts by n.

For an ionic compound such as Fe₂O₃, the empirical formula is the formula, because there are no separate molecules.
